BTC ⇄ ETH

Swap Bitcoin and ETH directly, in either direction, with a provider. No one holds your coins in between, and nothing is wrapped. The ETH you receive arrives private, inside Tacit’s confidential pool. This is a pilot: the code is new, it has had no independent audit yet, and each swap is capped while liquidity builds.

Use it

Get started

Go to tacit.finance/btc and sign in with your Ethereum wallet or a passkey. Signing in opens your Tacit key, which holds both your Bitcoin address and your private ETH. The same wallet or passkey always opens the same key, so you can sign in again on any device.

Pick a direction and an amount. The page asks the providers for a quote and shows it before you press: the market rate, the fee, the Bitcoin network fee, what you receive, how long it takes and who the provider is. A quote stands for about five minutes, and the page renews it while you look.

Bitcoin to ETH

  1. Choose “Receive as”. Keep the ETH as a private balance, or have it sent on to an Ethereum address once it has arrived.
  2. Press Swap. If a Bitcoin wallet is open in this browser, one payment from it starts the swap. If not, send the BTC to your Tacit Bitcoin address first (it is in your account menu and under “Your Bitcoin”), then press Swap.
  3. Wait for one Bitcoin block. Your page locks the BTC with one ordinary Bitcoin transaction. After its confirmations, the provider locks the same value in ETH in the pool for you, and your page claims it. Then the provider takes the BTC.
  4. Done. The ETH is in your private balance. You can send it on from “Your ETH”. The whole swap takes about 15–20 minutes.

ETH to Bitcoin

  1. Press Swap. If your private ETH balance is short, the page adds ETH from your wallet first: one transaction from your wallet adds it, then the swap starts. That deposit is public, but nothing on chain links it to the swap or to your Bitcoin address.
  2. The provider locks its BTC first. Your page waits for it to confirm: one block for smaller swaps, more for larger ones.
  3. You lock your ETH, the provider claims it, you take the BTC. Your page does the first and the last step.
  4. Done. The BTC is at your Tacit Bitcoin address (bc1q…), less the fee of the transaction that took it. From there you can send it to any wallet or exchange, use it on Tacit’s Bitcoin order book, or import its key into another wallet. The whole swap takes about 20–30 minutes.

Keep the page open until it says Done. Your browser does the signing, so it has to be there for the steps that need it. If you close it by mistake, see If something goes wrong.

Under “Your swaps” on the page, Back up saves your swaps to a file and Restore reads one back. Restore only a file you made yourself. The swaps can also be found again from your key alone, so the backup is a convenience, not a requirement.

Limits and fees

While liquidity builds, each swap has a floor and a cap. Both move with what the providers hold, and the page shows the live range under “Limits”.

DirectionLeastMost per swap
BTC to ETH0.00025 BTC0.01 BTC
ETH to BTC0.008 ETHWhat the providers hold in BTC at that moment

A BTC to ETH swap that is larger than one provider can fill may be split across up to four providers. The page shows the parts.

What a swap costs

  • The provider’s fee. A share of the amount, 0.20% at the provider Tacit runs, plus a flat part that covers its own Bitcoin costs. Each provider sets its own, and the page asks the cheapest first.
  • A lean. A provider that holds too much of one side moves its price a little to attract the other. The provider program allows up to 0.15% by default, and the one Tacit runs is set to up to 0.40%; it is never so far that the quote falls near the page’s 3% limit.
  • The Bitcoin network fee. In BTC to ETH, the fee of your lock transaction. In ETH to BTC, the fee of your take, which comes out of the BTC you receive. It is a few hundred sats at typical fee rates, about 500, and the quote shows it.

Fixed parts weigh most on small swaps. These figures are illustrative: they use the Tacit provider’s terms (0.20%, a flat 400 sats from BTC to ETH and 0.0001 ETH from ETH to BTC) and a network fee of about 500 sats, and take 30 ETH to a BTC. Real quotes differ.

How the price is checked

A provider prices from the market rate plus its fee. The page does not take that on trust. It reads Chainlink’s ETH/BTC price from at least two separate Ethereum nodes, all asked at the same block, and uses it only while they agree and the reading is fresh. It warns on a quote 2.5% under that price and blocks one more than 3% under it. If it cannot read the market, it blocks the quote, because a page that cannot tell cannot say a price is fair.

If something goes wrong

Closing a tab does not give anyone your funds. Each lock refunds on its own clock, and nothing leaves your control except into the swap’s lock. Here is what happens at each point.

Bitcoin to ETH

If you close the page…What happens
Before your BTC is sentNothing moved. Your BTC never left your address.
After your BTC is sent, before the provider locks its ETHReopen the page in the same browser and it carries on. On another device, it finds the swap again from your key and finishes it while the provider still holds its offer. If no one finishes, your BTC returns to your address after 144 blocks, about a day.
After the provider has locked its ETHCome back before the ETH deadline, about 4 hours after the lock. The page shows the time. If you do not, the ETH goes back to the provider and your BTC returns after about a day. You lose nothing.

ETH to Bitcoin

If you close the page…What happens
While the provider’s BTC confirmsNone of your ETH is locked yet. If you do not come back, the provider’s BTC returns to it.
After you lock your ETH, before the provider claimsYour ETH returns to your private balance after about 4 hours if the provider does not claim. A refund puts it back as private ETH.
After the provider has claimedThe BTC is yours from that moment. Take it before the provider’s refund opens, about 16 hours after its lock confirms. Your page also posts a take that is signed ahead, so a watcher can finish it for you. It can pay only your own address. The page says when your BTC is watched.

The one case where timing matters is the last one. If you are offline past the provider’s refund and no watcher takes for you, the provider can take its BTC back and keep your ETH. Coming back sooner is better.

Other cases

  • The provider goes offline. Before it has locked, nothing is at risk. After, each lock refunds as above.
  • The quote ran out. A swap starts only on a quote with more than two minutes left. If it ran out before your BTC was sent, nothing was sent. Ask for a new quote.
  • Bitcoin is slow. Blocks come about every ten minutes but sometimes much slower. After 15 minutes without a block the page says so. The Bitcoin refund counts blocks, so it waits for them.
  • You lose your device. Sign in with the same wallet or passkey. The page finds your swaps again from your key and the chains.

How it works

The swap’s path

Both directions follow one order. The side that locks Bitcoin holds a secret t and locks first. The other side locks only after that lock is confirmed. The side that holds t claims first on Ethereum, which shows t, and the other side uses it on Bitcoin.

Bitcoin to ETH

  1. You make t from your key and lock the BTC to a Taproot address keyed to the provider’s key plus T = t·G. The lock has a refund leaf that returns the BTC to you after 144 blocks.
  2. The provider reads your lock from Bitcoin, waits for its confirmations, and locks pool ETH of the quoted amount to T, with a deadline of about 4 hours and your claim key as the recipient. It sends you a pre-signature of the claim.
  3. Your browser checks the provider’s offer: the pre-signature, T, the recipient and the time left. Only then does it claim, completing the signature with t. The claim’s proof carries the signature z.
  4. The provider reads z, works out t = z − s̃, and takes your BTC.

ETH to Bitcoin

  1. The provider makes t and locks its BTC to a Taproot address keyed to your key plus T, refundable to it after a number of blocks, 96 (about 16 hours) in today’s ads.
  2. Your page reads that lock itself and waits for its confirmations. It checks the windows, then locks your pool ETH to T with a deadline of about 4 hours, the provider as the recipient.
  3. The provider claims your ETH. That shows z, and so t.
  4. You take the BTC with your key plus t.

Why the order is safe

The timelocks are set so that the Bitcoin refund opens at least two hours after the Ethereum deadline. The party that locks Ethereum then has time to see t and take the BTC before the other side could refund. A claim that is sent but does not land before its deadline still shows t, so the page refuses to build a claim with less than an hour left.

How it works

The lock and the secret

This is an adaptor-signature swap. Neither chain has to know about the other.

  • On Bitcoin, the lock is a Taproot output. Its key is the claimer’s key plus T, so spending it by the key path needs the claimer’s key and t. One script leaf lets the locker refund after a number of blocks (CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY, 144 blocks from you, 96 from a provider). The sats are plain sats, not a Tacit note.
  • On Ethereum, the pool has three operations, adaptorlock, adaptorclaim and adaptorrefund. A lock puts a note of the exact amount into the pool’s lock set under (T, deadline, recipient). A claim spends it to the recipient and needs a signature that is only whole when it includes t. A refund returns it to its owner after the deadline.
  • The link is t. The claimer pre-signs with T, so the pre-signature is not yet valid. Completing it takes t: z = s̃ + t. The claim’s proof carries z, so anyone reading the pool can work out t = z − s̃.

A pre-signature exists only if the locked note holds exactly the quoted amount. Nothing in an offer stands in for a chain read: each side takes the swap’s id, the lock’s script and the confirmations from the chains themselves.

Secrets come from your key

The secret, the blindings of the notes and a sealed hint in the Bitcoin lock are all derived from your Tacit key and the lock’s outpoint. So a swap can be found again from your key and the chains alone. That is how another device, or a browser with its storage cleared, picks a swap up.

The take that anyone can finish

In ETH to Bitcoin you take the BTC after the provider claims. If you are offline then, a watcher can do it for you. Your page signs the take ahead, except for the one number that needs t, and posts that pre-signature. It shows nothing without t, so it is safe to publish.

The signature uses SIGHASH_SINGLE | ANYONECANPAY. It fixes the lock as the input and your own address as the output, with the fee taken out. Anyone may add inputs to pay a higher fee, and no one can change where the sats go. When the provider claims, the watcher completes the signature with t, and the take pays your address. The API only holds these pre-signatures and checks each against the key that made it. The watcher checks again before it broadcasts.

How it works

The private pool

The ETH side lives in Tacit’s confidential pool on Ethereum. A balance there is a set of private notes. A note is a sealed record of an amount and its owner. The pool stores only the seal, called a commitment, in a Merkle tree of 32 levels. It keeps three such trees: notes, locks and positions.

To spend a note, you publish its nullifier. A nullifier is a tag only the owner can compute. It marks the note as spent, so it cannot be spent twice, and it does not say which note it was.

Each operation is proved with a zk proof. The proof shows that you own the notes and the amounts add up, without revealing either. The proofs are made with SP1 and wrapped in Groth16, so Ethereum checks each one cheaply. A relay posts them. The relay sees proofs, not keys, and cannot move your funds.

The swap uses the pool’s lock set: a lock is a note held under (T, deadline, recipient) until it is claimed or refunded. That is the Ethereum half of the swap.

How it works

What you trust

ForYou rely onNot on
Your fundsThe swap code in your browser, the proofs, and the pool’s contract codeThe provider, the relay or the provers
Your priceThe page’s own market check, from Chainlink on several nodesThe provider’s word
Your privacy on EthereumYour device, which makes the proofs of your own notesThe provider or the relay
  • Your browser checks the provider. Before it publishes anything that reveals t, it verifies the provider’s offer: the pre-signature, T, the recipient and the time left.
  • The provider cannot take both sides. The second locker never locks before the first lock is confirmed, and both locks open with the same secret.
  • The relay and prover cannot move your funds. They see proofs, not keys. Whoever proves a claim sees z before it lands, which is why the claim is not proved by the provider.
  • A modified provider could matter only in ETH to BTC, if you stay offline past the provider’s refund and no watcher takes for you.
  • ETH to BTC uses only providers the site names. In that direction the provider claims, and your BTC comes from reading its claim off the pool. Until that can be read from the pool’s own record, the page asks only providers it names. Other providers serve BTC to ETH only, until reviewed.
  • It is a pilot. The swap code is new and has had no independent audit. Swap sizes are capped, and you should not move more than you can afford to have delayed while you learn how it behaves.
  • Do not sign the Tacit sign-in message anywhere else. It is not tied to a website. It says to sign it only in a Tacit app you trust, and anyone who holds the signature controls your Tacit funds, including your swaps.

The pool’s proofs use pairings, like most zk systems on Ethereum, so they are not post-quantum.

More

Run a provider

Anyone can provide. A provider is a small process with its own key. It quotes from the market price plus its fee, and each swap locks both sides, so it never holds anyone’s funds. More providers add room to each swap and keep prices close.

What it needs

  • A key. Setup makes one and shows it once. Copy it somewhere offline: the funds are only as safe as that key.
  • ETH, shielded into the pool as the key’s private notes, to serve BTC to ETH. A small start is about 0.07 ETH, which covers swaps up to 0.001 BTC.
  • Optionally BTC at the provider’s own Bitcoin address, to serve ETH to BTC. Setup counts one confirmation.
  • A machine that stays on, with Node 22, or Docker. It must finish each swap before the user’s refund opens.

One command

git clone https://github.com/src-company/tacit && cd tacit
(cd worker-relay && npm ci --omit=dev --ignore-scripts) && (cd worker && npm ci --omit=dev --ignore-scripts)
node fastbridge-lp/setup.mjs

Setup asks for a name and your fee, prints the two addresses to fund, checks the nodes, the explorers, the price and a test quote, and starts. Stop it at any point and run it again: it picks up where it was.

How users find you

The provider signs an ad with its key: its terms, its limits and what it can lend now. It posts the ad to a board every minute, and each ad stands for a few minutes. The page reads the board and checks each signature itself. Your provider shows under “Providers” on tacit.finance/btc within a few minutes. You need no public address or domain.

Earn and risk

You earn a share of each swap (setup defaults to 0.30%) plus a flat part that covers Bitcoin fees. You risk capacity tied up while a swap is open, up to about 16 hours if a user disappears. You also pay network fees on both chains, and the price can move in that time. All of it is bounded by the most you allow per swap. Nothing here is a promise of income.

Questions

Does anyone hold my coins during a swap?

No. Your BTC is in a Bitcoin lock that you can refund. Your ETH is in a pool lock that you can refund. The provider’s coins are in the other lock of each pair. The lock opens only with the swap’s secret.

Is my ETH wrapped?

No. It is ETH in the pool, as private notes. You can send it on to any Ethereum address.

Can I get stuck?

Your coins can be late, but not lost. Each lock refunds after its timelock: about 4 hours for ETH, and about a day for Bitcoin you locked. See If something goes wrong.

Why does a small swap cost so much?

The Bitcoin network fee and the provider’s flat part are fixed. On a swap near the minimum they are a large share. Larger swaps cost close to the 0.20% fee.

Who sets the price?

The provider, from the market rate plus its fee. Your page checks it against Chainlink and blocks a quote more than 3% under.

Has it been audited?

Not yet by an independent party. The swap code is new, and the sizes are capped while it proves itself.

Can I swap without a Bitcoin wallet?

For BTC to ETH you need BTC at your Tacit Bitcoin address, from any wallet or exchange. For ETH to BTC you need only ETH.

Why only some providers for ETH to BTC?

There the provider makes the claim your BTC depends on. The page asks only providers the site names until a claim can be read from the pool’s own record.

Words used here

Provider
A process with a key that takes the other side of a swap and earns a fee.
Atomic swap
A swap in which both sides complete or both refund.
Adaptor signature
A signature made incomplete on purpose. A secret t completes it, and the completed signature shows t.
Taproot
Bitcoin’s address type that can hide a script. A lock is a Taproot output.
CSV timelock
A rule that lets a refund be spent only after a number of blocks.
Pre-signature
An adaptor signature a party hands over before it can be used.
Note
A sealed record of an amount and its owner. A private balance is a set of notes.
Nullifier
A tag that marks a note as spent without saying which note it was.
Watcher
A process that finishes a user’s take from a signed-ahead pre-signature, and can pay only the user’s own address.
Board
A small service where providers post signed ads and users find them.
